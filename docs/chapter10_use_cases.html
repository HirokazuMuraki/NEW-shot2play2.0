Chapter 10 — Use Cases

This chapter defines representative use cases for the NEW-shot2play Protocol Suite Version 2.0.

The use cases demonstrate how Authentication, Entitlement, Policy Evaluation, Authorization, and Service Enforcement operate as a coordinated protocol framework.

The examples in this chapter are representative applications of the protocol model and do not limit the protocol to the specific services, resources, transactions, or business conditions described herein.

10.1 Purpose and Scope

The purpose of this chapter is to demonstrate representative operational scenarios enabled by the Version 2.0 protocol architecture.

Each use case identifies the relevant protocol state and illustrates how that state contributes to an Authorization Decision and subsequent Service execution.

The use cases are intended to clarify the interaction between Authentication, Entitlement, Policy Evaluation, Authorization, and Service Enforcement.

10.2 Basic Authorization Flow

A subject first establishes an authenticated identity. The system then determines whether an applicable Entitlement exists and evaluates the conditions defined by the applicable Policy.

The resulting information is used to establish an Authorization Decision.

The protected Service executes the requested operation only when an effective Allow decision exists.

10.3 Authentication Does Not Constitute Authorization

Successful Authentication establishes the authenticated state of a subject but does not, by itself, establish permission to perform a protected Service operation.

Authorization MUST be evaluated independently of the fact that the subject has successfully authenticated.

An authenticated subject MAY therefore be denied access when no applicable Entitlement exists or when the applicable Policy conditions are not satisfied.

10.4 Entitlement-Based Service Access

A subject MAY possess an Entitlement permitting access to a specified Service, resource, or operation.

The Entitlement MAY define the permitted scope, validity period, usage conditions, transaction restrictions, or other Policy-defined constraints.

The Authorization Decision MUST be based on the effective Entitlement and the applicable Policy.

10.5 Time-Limited Entitlement

An Entitlement MAY define an effective time and an expiration time.

Before the effective time, the Entitlement MUST NOT be treated as effective.

After expiration, the Entitlement MUST NOT be used as the basis for authorizing a protected operation.

Where an expired Entitlement is the only applicable authorization basis, the Authorization Decision MUST be Deny.

10.6 Conditional Entitlement

An Entitlement MAY become effective only when one or more conditions defined by Policy are satisfied.

Such conditions MAY include time, location, transaction state, resource state, prior activity, or another defined contextual condition.

The existence of an Entitlement alone does not establish that its conditions have been satisfied.

10.7 Policy-Dependent Entitlement

The applicability of an Entitlement MAY be determined by evaluating the Entitlement together with a Policy.

The Policy Evaluation process MUST determine whether the Entitlement applies to the current request and authorization context.

The same Entitlement MAY therefore result in different Authorization Decisions when the applicable Policy conditions or context differ.

10.8 Visit-Based Entitlement

A Service MAY establish an Entitlement as a result of a defined real-world or digital event.

For example, a subject who completes a visit authentication MAY receive an Entitlement permitting a discount on a subsequent e-commerce transaction.

The resulting Entitlement MAY specify the applicable Service, validity period, transaction scope, or usage restriction.

10.9 Entitlement and Transaction Binding

An Entitlement MAY be bound to a specific transaction.

When transaction binding is required, the Authorization process MUST verify that the Entitlement applies to the transaction being processed.

An Entitlement established for one transaction MUST NOT automatically authorize another transaction unless the applicable Policy explicitly permits such reuse.

10.10 Entitlement with Usage Limit

An Entitlement MAY specify a maximum permitted usage count.

When a protected operation consumes permitted usage, the corresponding usage state MUST be updated according to the applicable Policy.

After the permitted usage count has been exhausted, subsequent operations MUST NOT be authorized by that Entitlement.

10.11 Policy Evaluation Before Authorization

Before an Authorization Decision is generated, the applicable Policy MUST be evaluated against the current authorization context.

The evaluation MAY consider the subject, Entitlement, requested Service, requested operation, resource, transaction state, time, and other Policy-defined conditions.

An Authorization Decision MUST NOT be generated as an Allow decision when a mandatory Policy condition is not satisfied.

10.12 Context-Dependent Authorization

An Authorization Decision MAY depend on contextual information that is independent of the authenticated identity itself.

For example, an Entitlement MAY permit an operation only during a defined time interval or only when a specified transaction state exists.

The Authorization process MUST evaluate the context required by the applicable Policy.

10.13 Service-Specific Policy

Different Services MAY apply different Policies to the same authenticated subject or Entitlement.

An Entitlement that is valid for one Service MUST NOT automatically authorize access to another Service unless the applicable Policy permits such use.

The Authorization Decision MUST therefore include the Service scope applicable to the request.

10.14 Operation-Specific Authorization

An Entitlement MAY permit one operation while prohibiting another operation against the same resource.

For example, an Entitlement MAY permit viewing a resource while prohibiting modification or deletion of that resource.

The Authorization Decision MUST consider the requested operation as part of the effective authorization scope.

10.15 Resource-Specific Authorization

An Authorization Decision MAY be restricted to a particular resource or resource class.

The resource scope MUST be explicitly represented or otherwise determinable from the applicable authorization context.

Authorization for one resource MUST NOT automatically authorize access to another resource unless such inheritance is explicitly permitted by Policy.

10.16 Session Authorization Continuity

An Authorization Decision MAY remain applicable during a Session after the original Authentication operation has completed.

Where authorization continuity is permitted, the Authorization state MUST remain associated with the applicable Session and authorization context.

A Service MUST NOT treat the mere existence of a Session as unrestricted authorization.

10.17 Authorization Decision Expiration

An Authorization Decision MAY have a validity period independent of the lifetime of the authenticated Session.

When the Authorization Decision expires, it MUST NOT continue to authorize a protected operation.

Where continued access is permitted by Policy, a new Authorization Decision MUST be established.

10.18 Authorization Revocation

An Entitlement, Policy condition, or other authorization basis MAY be revoked before its originally defined expiration time.

When revocation invalidates an existing Authorization Decision, subsequent protected operations MUST be denied unless another valid authorization basis exists.

Revocation MUST be applied at the applicable authorization enforcement boundary.

10.19 Fail-Closed Authorization

When a required authorization state cannot be established as valid, the Service MUST fail closed when required by Policy.

Examples include an unavailable authorization state, an unverifiable authorization binding, an expired decision, or an unresolved revocation state.

Failure to obtain sufficient information for an Allow decision MUST NOT be interpreted as permission to execute the protected operation.

10.20 Authorization Decision Binding

An Authorization Decision MAY be bound to the subject, Entitlement, Policy version, Service, operation, resource, transaction, or other authorization context used to generate the decision.

When such binding is required, the Service MUST verify that the request remains within the scope of the Authorization Decision.

A request that falls outside the established binding MUST NOT be authorized using the existing decision.

10.21 Cross-Service Entitlement

An Entitlement MAY be applicable to more than one Service.

When an Entitlement is shared across Services, the applicable Service scope MUST be explicitly represented or determinable from the applicable Policy.

Each Service MUST independently enforce the authorization scope applicable to its own protected operations.

10.22 Common Entitlement with Service-Specific Policy

Multiple Services MAY rely on a common Entitlement while applying different Policies to their respective operations.

A common Entitlement therefore does not require identical Authorization Decisions across all Services.

Each Service MUST evaluate the Policy applicable to its own authorization request.

10.23 Cross-Service Authorization Context

Authorization context MAY be established by one Service and subsequently referenced by another Service when permitted by Policy.

The receiving Service MUST verify the integrity and applicability of the referenced context before using it for authorization.

Context established for one Service MUST NOT automatically be trusted by another Service without an applicable trust or validation rule.

10.24 Cross-Service Entitlement Transfer

An Entitlement MAY permit a subject to access a second Service after a qualifying event has been established by a first Service.

For example, a visit authentication performed by one Service MAY establish an Entitlement that permits a discount operation in an e-commerce Service.

The second Service MUST evaluate the transferred authorization context according to its applicable Policy.

10.25 Cross-Service State Continuity

Authorization state MAY remain effective while a subject moves between Services when the applicable Policy permits continuity.

The authorization state MUST remain associated with the identity, Entitlement, Policy, and other context required to preserve its meaning.

A Service MUST NOT assume authorization continuity when required state cannot be established or validated.

10.26 Distributed Authorization

Authorization Decisions MAY be generated or enforced across multiple distributed components.

Each component participating in authorization MUST operate on an authorization state that is consistent with the applicable protocol and Policy.

Distributed processing MUST NOT result in an authorization scope broader than the effective authorization state.

10.27 Authorization State Reconstruction

A Service MAY reconstruct authorization state after a restart, failover, or other loss of transient execution state.

Reconstructed state MUST be derived from authoritative protocol objects or other trusted state defined by Policy.

A Service MUST NOT treat incomplete or unverifiable reconstructed state as sufficient for an Allow decision when the applicable Policy requires complete authorization state.

10.28 Authorization Decision Caching

A Service MAY cache an Authorization Decision to reduce repeated evaluation.

A cached decision MUST remain subject to the validity period, authorization scope, Policy version, and revocation conditions that governed the original decision.

A cached decision MUST NOT be used after its effective validity has expired or when Policy requires re-evaluation.

10.29 Risk- or Context-Dependent Authorization

A Policy MAY cause the Authorization Decision to depend on a risk value or other dynamically evaluated context.

For example, a Service MAY require additional conditions before permitting a high-risk operation even when the subject possesses a valid Entitlement.

The resulting Authorization Decision MUST reflect the effective Policy and the evaluated context.

10.30 Emergency Authorization

A Policy MAY define an emergency authorization mechanism for circumstances in which ordinary authorization conditions cannot be satisfied through normal operation.

Emergency authorization MUST be explicitly distinguishable from ordinary authorization.

The emergency authorization MAY be restricted by time, scope, operation, resource, usage count, or other Policy-defined conditions.

When the emergency condition terminates, the emergency authorization MUST cease to provide authorization.

10.31 Use Case Summary

The use cases defined in this chapter demonstrate that Authentication, Entitlement, Policy Evaluation, Authorization, and Service Enforcement form distinct but coordinated stages of the Version 2.0 protocol framework.

Authentication establishes the authenticated state of the subject. Entitlement establishes the authorization basis. Policy Evaluation determines the applicability of that basis under current conditions. Authorization produces the effective decision, and Service Enforcement applies that decision to the protected operation.

The use cases further demonstrate that authorization MAY depend on time, transaction state, resource scope, Service scope, cross-Service context, usage limits, revocation state, and other Policy-defined conditions.

These conditions MAY change after Authentication has completed. Accordingly, the authorization state MUST remain independently manageable from the authentication state.

The examples also demonstrate that authorization state MAY be maintained, transferred, reconstructed, cached, revoked, or re-evaluated according to Policy while remaining subject to Service enforcement and fail-closed requirements.
